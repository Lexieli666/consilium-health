> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Screening

Preventive-services guidance describes screening adults for anxiety disorders where systems are in
place to ensure accurate diagnosis and follow-up, with the strongest statement made for adults under
65. For older adults the evidence is judged insufficient to make a recommendation either way — a
statement about the evidence, not a recommendation against screening, and one that is frequently
misread as the latter.

The seven-item generalized anxiety disorder questionnaire (generalised in British spelling) is the
instrument most often used, both to screen and to track severity. The commonly used severity bands
are 5 for mild, 10 for moderate and 15 for severe, with 10 the threshold at which further assessment
usually follows. A questionnaire score is not a diagnosis: it prompts a diagnostic assessment, which
includes ruling out physical causes such as thyroid disease and arrhythmia, reviewing substance,
caffeine and medication effects, and assessing for co-occurring depression, which is common enough
that finding one is a reason to look for the other.

## First-line treatment

Two first-line options of broadly comparable effectiveness, chosen with the person rather than for
them:

- **Cognitive behavioral therapy**, delivered individually, in a group, or through a guided digital
  program, with the largest evidence base of the psychological therapies.
- **Pharmacotherapy** with a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor or a serotonin-noradrenaline
  reuptake inhibitor.

Combining the two is usual for more severe or non-responding presentations. Availability, waiting
times and preference legitimately drive the choice, since a treatment that starts is better than a
better treatment that does not.

## Timing of review

Response is reviewed at four to six weeks after medication starts, with the caveat that anxiety
symptoms can transiently worsen in the first days — a predictable effect worth warning about in
advance, because it is a common reason for stopping early. Eight to twelve weeks at an adequate dose
precedes any conclusion that an agent has failed. Where a first agent fails, switching within or
between classes comes before adding further agents.

## Duration and stopping

Where treatment is effective, it continues for six to twelve months before a gradual taper is
considered, because early discontinuation is associated with relapse. Tapering is gradual and
monitored rather than abrupt, and discontinuation symptoms are distinguished from a return of the
underlying disorder, which they closely resemble and which they precede in time.

## Benzodiazepines

Benzodiazepines are not first-line for generalized anxiety disorder and are inappropriate for
long-term use: tolerance, dependence, cognitive effects, falls in older adults, and no effect on the
underlying disorder. Where used at all, the role is short and bounded.

## What is outside this note

Doses and prescription decisions are outside the scope of this note and of this system. Thoughts of
self-harm or suicide are an urgent situation covered by the emergency guidance elsewhere in this
corpus, not by routine review.
