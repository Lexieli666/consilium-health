## The code

Essential, or primary, hypertension is I10 in ICD-10-CM. It sits in Chapter 9, diseases of the
circulatory system, in the hypertensive-disease block that runs from I10 to I16.

I10 is unusual in being a complete code at three characters. Most ICD-10-CM categories require a
fourth, fifth or sixth character before the code is billable; I10 takes none, so a request to
"add more specificity to I10" is a request the classification cannot satisfy. What the category
includes is broad: high blood pressure described as arterial, essential, primary or systemic all map
to the same code. The benign-versus-malignant split that the previous US classification carried was
not brought forward, which is why searching for a "malignant hypertension" code returns nothing
separate.

## What I10 is not used for

A single raised reading, or a series of readings, in someone who has not been given a diagnosis of
hypertension is a finding rather than a disease, and it is classified with the abnormal findings in
Chapter 18 instead. The distinction is documentation-driven: the diagnosis in the record, not the
number, is what moves a case to I10. The same chapter holds the code for low blood pressure.

Hypertension complicating pregnancy, childbirth or the period after delivery is excluded outright
and is classified in the obstetric chapter; the pregnancy code is used in place of I10, not
alongside it. Hypertension affecting the vessels of the brain or of the eye is handled differently
again — those are separate conditions that may be coded in addition to the hypertension rather than
instead of it.

## When hypertension stops being I10

I10 applies when hypertension stands alone. Once organ involvement is in the picture, the
classification prefers a combination code:

- Documented hypertensive heart disease moves the case to I11, subdivided by whether heart failure
  is present. When it is, the type of heart failure is added from I50 as a second code.
- Hypertension with chronic kidney disease moves to I12, with a second code identifying the stage of
  kidney disease.
- Both together move to I13.
- Hypertension attributed to an identifiable underlying cause — renal, endocrine, or another — is
  secondary hypertension in I15, coded together with the cause.
- Hypertensive urgency and hypertensive emergency are distinguished within I16.

The heart and kidney rules differ in an important way. The classification presumes a causal link
between hypertension and chronic kidney disease and pairs them without being told to. It makes no
such presumption for heart disease: unless the record states that the heart condition is
hypertensive or due to the hypertension, the two are coded separately and I11 is not used.

## Related codes that appear on the same encounter

Screening for cardiovascular disorders in someone without symptoms has its own encounter code in the
factors-influencing-health chapter, as does a personal history of, or long-term drug therapy for, a
circulatory condition. Tobacco use or dependence is frequently added, because many categories in
Chapter 9 carry an explicit instruction to identify it.
