## The range and what it holds

Chapter 6 of ICD-10-CM covers G00 through G99: diseases of the nervous system, central and
peripheral, together with the neuromuscular junction and muscle. Every code begins with the letter
G. The chapter is organized by mechanism rather than by anatomy, which is why the block boundaries
are not obvious from a symptom.

## The blocks, in order

Inflammatory diseases of the central nervous system open the chapter at G00 to G09 — the
meningitides classified by organism, encephalitis, and intracranial abscess. Systemic atrophies
primarily affecting the central nervous system follow at G10 to G14.

Extrapyramidal and movement disorders occupy G20 to G26, including Parkinson disease, the dystonias,
tremor and restless legs. Other degenerative diseases of the nervous system sit at G30 to G32, which
is where Alzheimer disease is classified, and the demyelinating diseases including multiple
sclerosis at G35 to G37.

The block that carries most everyday questions is G40 to G47, episodic and paroxysmal disorders. It
holds epilepsy and seizures, migraine, the other headache syndromes such as cluster and tension-type
headache, transient cerebral ischemic attack, and the sleep disorders of organic origin — insomnia,
hypersomnia, circadian rhythm disorders, sleep apnea (apnoea) and the parasomnias.

Nerve, nerve root and plexus disorders follow at G50 to G59, covering the cranial neuropathies
including facial palsy, and the entrapment neuropathies. Polyneuropathies and other disorders of the
peripheral nervous system occupy G60 to G65, diseases of the myoneural junction and muscle G70 to
G73, and cerebral palsy and the other paralytic syndromes G80 to G83. The chapter closes at G89 to
G99 with a mixed block that includes the pain categories and postprocedural nervous-system
disorders.

## What the chapter does not hold, and where those codes live

Stroke is the boundary people cross by accident. Cerebral infarction and intracranial hemorrhage are
cerebrovascular disease and are classified with the circulatory system, and so are the codes for
deficits persisting afterwards — but transient ischemic attack is here, in G45. The two sit in
different chapters despite being discussed together.

Headache is the other boundary. A headache syndrome with a name is a G code; headache as an
undiagnosed symptom is classified in Chapter 18, along with dizziness, somnolence, abnormal
involuntary movements, convulsions not identified as epilepsy, and an abnormal neurological finding
with no diagnosis. Sleep disorders that are not attributed to a substance or a known physiological
condition are classified with the mental and behavioral disorders instead of here.

## Codes from other chapters that nervous-system questions commonly need

Diabetic neuropathy is coded from the diabetes category in the endocrine chapter, not from the
polyneuropathy block, because the classification presumes the association. Traumatic brain and
spinal injury belongs to the injury chapter, with this chapter holding the sequelae only where a
named neurological condition results. Long-term drug therapy status and family history come from the
factors-influencing-health chapter.
