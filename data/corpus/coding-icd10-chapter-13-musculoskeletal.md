## The range and what it holds

Chapter 13 of ICD-10-CM covers M00 through M99: diseases of the musculoskeletal system and
connective tissue. Every code begins with the letter M. Two features run through the whole chapter
and distinguish it from most others — site is carried in the code itself rather than in a separate
one, and most site-specific categories carry laterality, so right, left and unspecified are
different codes.

## The blocks, in order

Infectious arthropathies open the chapter at M00 to M02, followed at M04 by the autoinflammatory
syndromes and at M05 to M14 by the inflammatory polyarthropathies, where rheumatoid arthritis and
gout are classified.

Osteoarthritis occupies M15 to M19 as a block of its own, subdivided first by joint and then, within
a joint, by cause and laterality. Other joint disorders follow at M20 to M25 — deformities,
internal derangements, and the joint pain, stiffness and effusion categories. Dentofacial anomalies
and jaw disorders sit at M26 to M27.

Systemic connective tissue disorders occupy M30 to M36, which is where the vasculitides and lupus
are classified, and where questions about arteritis land despite the arteries themselves belonging
to the circulatory chapter.

The spine blocks follow: deforming dorsopathies at M40 to M43, spondylopathies at M45 to M49
including ankylosing spondylitis and spinal stenosis, and other dorsopathies at M50 to M54, which
holds the cervical and lumbar disc disorders, radiculopathy, and low back and neck pain.

Soft tissue occupies M60 to M79 — muscle disorders, then the synovial and tendon disorders including
bursitis and tendinitis, then a long residual block covering fibromyalgia, fasciitis and pain in a
limb. Disorders of bone density and structure follow at M80 to M85, where osteoporosis is
classified with and without a current pathological fracture. Other osteopathies occupy M86 to M90,
chondropathies M91 to M94, and the chapter closes at M95 to M99 with acquired deformities,
postprocedural musculoskeletal disorders and the biomechanical lesion categories.

## What the chapter does not hold, and where those codes live

Injury is the boundary that matters. A traumatic fracture, sprain, strain or dislocation is
classified in the injury chapter with a seventh character recording the episode of care, while a
pathological fracture — bone that broke because it was diseased — stays in this chapter. Which
chapter a fracture belongs to therefore depends on the documentation, not on the bone.

Congenital deformities and malformations belong to the congenital chapter, and remain there in
adults. Neoplasms of bone, cartilage and connective tissue are neoplasms. Musculoskeletal symptoms
that carry no diagnosis, such as an abnormal gait or difficulty walking, are classified in Chapter
18.

## Codes from other chapters that musculoskeletal questions commonly need

Body mass index accompanies obesity-related joint disease and is coded from the
factors-influencing-health chapter, along with long-term drug therapy status, presence of a joint
prosthesis, and screening encounters. External-cause codes from Chapter 20 describe how an injury
happened and are reported in addition to the injury code rather than instead of it. Diabetes and
psoriasis both carry arthropathy manifestations coded from their own chapters.
