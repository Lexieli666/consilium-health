## The range and what it holds

Chapter 4 of ICD-10-CM covers E00 through E89 and takes in three things that are grouped together
more by tradition than by mechanism: disorders of the endocrine glands, nutritional disorders, and
inherited and acquired disorders of metabolism. Every code begins with the letter E.

## The blocks, in order

Disorders of the thyroid gland open the chapter at E00 to E07, running from the iodine-deficiency
states through hypothyroidism, thyrotoxicosis, thyroiditis and goiter to the residual categories.

Diabetes mellitus occupies E08 to E13, and the split within it is by cause rather than by treatment:
diabetes due to an underlying condition, drug- or chemical-induced diabetes, type 1, type 2, and
other specified diabetes each hold a separate three-character category. E15 to E16 continue with the
other disorders of glucose regulation and of pancreatic internal secretion, including the
hypoglycemia categories used when diabetes is not the cause.

Disorders of other endocrine glands follow at E20 to E35 — parathyroid, pituitary, adrenal, ovarian
and testicular dysfunction, and the polyglandular syndromes.

The nutritional blocks come next. Malnutrition occupies E40 to E46, other nutritional deficiencies
E50 to E64, and overweight, obesity and other hyperalimentation E65 to E68. Metabolic disorders make
up the long final block at E70 to E88: the inherited disorders of amino-acid, carbohydrate and lipid
metabolism, disorders of mineral metabolism, cystic fibrosis, amyloidosis, volume depletion and the
fluid, electrolyte and acid-base disturbances. A short closing block at E89 covers endocrine and
metabolic complications following a procedure.

## What the chapter does not hold, and where those codes live

Transitory endocrine and metabolic disorders specific to the newborn are classified in the perinatal
chapter rather than here. Endocrine disorders arising in pregnancy, including gestational diabetes,
belong to the obstetric chapter. Neoplasms of endocrine glands are neoplasms, though the functional
disturbance a tumor causes may be coded from this chapter in addition.

The boundary with abnormal findings is the one that catches people. Prediabetes, impaired fasting
glucose, impaired glucose tolerance and an isolated raised or lowered glucose result with no
diagnosis recorded are all abnormal laboratory findings, and they are classified in Chapter 18 —
not in the diabetes block. An abnormal thyroid function test without a diagnosis sits in Chapter 18
for the same reason.

## Codes from other chapters that endocrine questions commonly need

Body mass index has its own category in the factors-influencing-health chapter and is reported
alongside an obesity diagnosis rather than in place of one. Long-term use of insulin, of injectable
non-insulin agents and of oral glucose-lowering drugs is coded from that chapter too, as are
screening encounters and family history. Diabetic complication codes reach outward constantly: stage
of chronic kidney disease from the genitourinary chapter, ulcer site and depth from the skin
chapter, and retinal detail from the eye chapter.
