> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Severe low blood sugar — call emergency services immediately

- Unconsciousness, seizure, or inability to swallow safely.
- Confusion or behavior change severe enough that the person cannot treat themselves.
- Any low blood sugar in someone who does not improve after taking fast-acting carbohydrate.

Someone who is unconscious is not given anything by mouth. Where an emergency glucagon kit or nasal
glucagon is available and someone present knows how to use it, guidance is that it is given while
emergency services are on the way, and the person is placed in the recovery position.

**Milder low blood sugar** — shakiness, sweating, hunger, palpitations, anxiety, difficulty
concentrating, irritability — is treated promptly with fast-acting carbohydrate, then rechecked, and
followed by longer-acting carbohydrate. Anyone using insulin or an agent that stimulates insulin
release should have a plan for this, and repeated episodes are a reason to review the regimen rather
than to keep treating the episodes.

## Very high blood sugar with these features — call emergency services immediately

- Nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain.
- Deep, rapid or labored breathing, or breath with a fruity or acetone smell.
- Drowsiness, confusion, or difficulty staying awake.
- Severe dehydration, very dry mouth, or not passing urine.
- Rapid breathing and weakness in someone with type 1 diabetes, or anyone unable to keep fluids
  down.

These suggest diabetic ketoacidosis or a hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state, both of which develop
over hours to days and are life-threatening.

## Two points that are commonly missed

**Ketoacidosis can occur at near-normal glucose levels.** This is described particularly with
SGLT-2 inhibitors, and it is dangerous precisely because a reassuring glucose reading leads people
and clinicians to look elsewhere. Vomiting, abdominal pain and rapid breathing in someone taking one
of these agents warrant assessment regardless of the glucose value.

**Illness raises requirements rather than lowering them.** Insulin is not stopped because the person
is not eating during an illness — the combination of infection and stopped insulin is a classic
route into ketoacidosis. Anyone on insulin should have sick-day guidance agreed in advance with
their own clinician; this system does not provide it.

## Hypoglycemia unawareness

Repeated low episodes blunt the warning symptoms, so someone can progress to severe hypoglycemia
without the early signs. This is a specific reason for urgent review of a regimen, and it affects
driving safety.

This system does not diagnose and does not give dosing instructions. Any presentation matching this
note is escalated, and no answer it produces is a substitute for emergency assessment.
