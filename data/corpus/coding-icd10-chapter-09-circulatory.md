## The range and what it holds

Chapter 9 of ICD-10-CM covers I00 through I99. Every code begins with the letter I. It takes in the
heart, the arteries and veins, the lymphatic vessels and lymph nodes, and the blood supply to the
brain. Its blocks run in anatomical and mechanistic order rather
than alphabetical, so codes sitting near each other describe related processes and knowing where a
block begins narrows a lookup.

## The blocks, in order

Acute rheumatic fever opens the chapter at I00 to I02, followed by chronic rheumatic heart disease
at I05 to I09 — the valve damage, mostly mitral and aortic, left behind by earlier infection.

Hypertensive disease occupies I10 to I16, a short block carrying combination codes that fold
hypertension together with heart or kidney disease attributed to it.

Ischemic heart disease — coronary artery disease — runs from I20 to I25, moving from angina through
acute myocardial infarction to the chronic atherosclerotic disease that follows. Pulmonary heart disease and disorders of the
pulmonary circulation sit at I26 to I28: pulmonary embolism, pulmonary hypertension, and the
right-sided heart disease that follows lung disease.

The largest block, I30 to I52, is other forms of heart disease, heterogeneous by design —
inflammatory disease of the pericardium, endocardium and myocardium; non-rheumatic valve disorders;
cardiomyopathy; the conduction disorders and the arrhythmias, atrial fibrillation among them;
cardiac arrest; and heart failure at the end of the block.

Cerebrovascular disease is I60 to I69, ordered by mechanism: subarachnoid and intracerebral bleeding
first, then infarction, then occlusion and stenosis, closing with the sequelae category for
deficits persisting after the event.

Diseases of the arteries, arterioles and capillaries follow at I70 to I79 — atherosclerosis
identified by site, aneurysm and dissection, peripheral arterial disease. Veins, lymphatic vessels
and lymph nodes occupy I80 to I89: thrombosis and phlebitis, varicose veins, lymphedema. A short
closing block at I95 to I99 holds low blood pressure, post-procedural disorders, and the
unspecified residual.

## What the chapter does not hold, and where those codes live

Transient ischemic attack is classified with the nervous system rather than with cerebrovascular
disease — the most common misplacement in this area. Congenital malformations of the heart and great
vessels belong to the congenital chapter, and stay there for an adult. Circulatory disorders
complicating pregnancy or childbirth are classified in the obstetric chapter. Giant cell arteritis
and the systemic vasculitides sit with the connective-tissue disorders in the musculoskeletal
chapter.

Symptoms are the other boundary: chest pain, palpitations, an abnormal cardiac finding and a raised
blood-pressure reading in someone with no diagnosis stay with the symptoms and abnormal findings in
Chapter 18 until a diagnosis is recorded.

## Codes from other chapters that circulatory questions commonly need

Many categories here carry an explicit instruction to add a code identifying tobacco use,
dependence or exposure, split between the mental and behavioral chapter and the
factors-influencing-health chapter. Stage of chronic kidney disease comes from the genitourinary
chapter and diabetes from the endocrine chapter. Long-term drug therapy, body mass index, family
history and screening encounters all come from the factors-influencing-health chapter.
