## The range and what it holds

Chapter 10 of ICD-10-CM covers J00 through J99, diseases of the respiratory system from the nose to
the pleura. Every code begins with the letter J. The chapter opens with the infections, ordered
roughly from the upper airway downward, then moves to the chronic airway diseases, then to lung
damage caused from outside the body.

## The blocks, in order

Acute upper respiratory infections occupy J00 to J06 — the common cold, sinusitis, pharyngitis,
tonsillitis, laryngitis and the categories for infections of multiple or unspecified upper sites.
Influenza and pneumonia follow at J09 to J18, with influenza divided by whether the virus was
identified and, if so, which kind, and pneumonia divided by causative organism. Other acute lower
respiratory infections, including acute bronchitis and bronchiolitis, sit at J20 to J22.

Other diseases of the upper respiratory tract occupy J30 to J39, covering allergic rhinitis, chronic
sinusitis, nasal polyps and the vocal cord and laryngeal disorders.

Chronic lower respiratory diseases are the block most questions land in, at J40 to J47. Bronchitis
not specified as acute or chronic opens it, followed by chronic bronchitis, emphysema, chronic
obstructive pulmonary disease, asthma and bronchiectasis in that order.

Lung diseases due to external agents follow at J60 to J70 — the pneumoconioses, hypersensitivity
pneumonitis, chemical and radiation injury, and aspiration pneumonitis. Other respiratory diseases
principally affecting the interstitium occupy J80 to J84, which is where acute respiratory distress
syndrome and pulmonary fibrosis are classified. Suppurative and necrotic conditions of the lower
respiratory tract sit at J85 to J86, diseases of the pleura at J90 to J94, and intraoperative and
postprocedural respiratory complications at J95. The chapter closes at J96 to J99 with respiratory
failure and the residual categories.

## What the chapter does not hold, and where those codes live

Obstructive sleep apnea (apnoea) is classified with the nervous system, not here, which surprises
people who expect it beside the other breathing disorders. Pulmonary embolism and pulmonary
hypertension are circulatory disease. Tuberculosis and most other specified infections named by
organism are classified in the infectious-disease chapter, and this chapter's pneumonia categories
carry instructions pointing there. Neoplasms of the lung are neoplasms; congenital malformations of
the respiratory tract belong to the congenital chapter.

Symptoms are the other boundary. Cough, shortness of breath, wheezing, hemoptysis, hypoxemia,
abnormal breathing and an abnormal chest imaging finding with no diagnosis recorded are all
classified in Chapter 18 rather than here.

## Codes from other chapters that respiratory questions commonly need

Tobacco is the constant. Categories throughout this chapter carry an explicit instruction to add a
code identifying tobacco dependence, exposure, use or past history, and those codes are split
between the mental and behavioral chapter and the factors-influencing-health chapter. Occupational
and environmental exposure codes come from the factors-influencing-health chapter, as do
vaccination-status and screening encounters, and the external-cause chapter supplies the agent when
a chemical or fume is responsible.
