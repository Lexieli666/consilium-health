> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## What it is

Generalized anxiety disorder (generalised in British spelling) is persistent, excessive worry about
a range of everyday matters, occurring more days than not for at least six months, which the person
finds difficult to control and which is accompanied by physical and cognitive symptoms. The
distinguishing features are the breadth of the worry — it moves from topic to topic rather than
attaching to one situation — and the impairment it causes.

Anxiety itself is a normal and useful response to threat. What makes it a disorder is that it is
disproportionate to the circumstances, persistent when the circumstances change, and costly to
functioning.

## How it is usually recognized

The worry is accompanied by restlessness, being easily fatigued, difficulty concentrating,
irritability, muscle tension, and disturbed sleep. People frequently present with the physical
symptoms rather than the worry — palpitations, chest tightness, breathlessness, headaches,
gastrointestinal upset, dizziness — and are investigated for a physical cause first. That sequence is
appropriate the first time, since thyroid disease, arrhythmia, and stimulant or medication effects
genuinely produce these symptoms, but repeating it indefinitely delays the diagnosis.

Screening instruments and severity bands are covered by the anxiety guideline note in this corpus.

## What contributes to it

A combination of genetic predisposition, temperament, adverse childhood experience, chronic stress,
and physical illness. Caffeine, alcohol withdrawal, nicotine withdrawal, some medications, and
stimulant use can produce or worsen the symptoms, and they are worth reviewing before anything else
is concluded. Depression co-occurs frequently enough that finding one is a reason to assess for the
other, and the combination affects treatment choice.

## Why it is treated

Untreated, it tends to run a chronic, fluctuating course. It is associated with impaired work and
relationships, increased use of medical services and investigation, poorer outcomes in coexisting
physical illness, and increased risk of depression and substance use. It also responds well to
treatment — which is the more important point, since the condition's own logic tells the person that
nothing will help.

## How it is generally managed

Cognitive behavioral therapy or medication as first-line options of comparable effectiveness,
combined for more severe presentations, alongside sleep, activity, caffeine and alcohol review.
Self-management approaches with evidence include structured exercise, relaxation and mindfulness
practice, and guided digital programs. Treatment selection, review intervals and duration are
covered by the anxiety guideline note in this corpus.

## When urgent assessment is advised

Thoughts of suicide or self-harm require urgent help rather than a routine appointment, and are
covered by the emergency notes in this corpus. A first panic attack with chest pain, breathlessness
or collapse is assessed as a physical emergency until a cardiac and respiratory cause has been
excluded — the two are not distinguishable by how they feel, and a person with an anxiety diagnosis
can also have a cardiac event.
