## The shape of current guidance

Older guidance set out a single sequence: metformin first, then agents added in order as control
worsened. Current guidance is structured differently — the first-line choice depends on which other
conditions are present, and for several of them the choice is made independently of the HbA1c value.
This is the substantive change, and it is the part most often stated in the old form.

## Where there is no compelling comorbidity

Metformin, alongside structured lifestyle intervention, is the usual initial agent: long experience,
low cost, weight neutrality, and little risk of causing low blood sugar on its own. Kidney function
is checked before starting and periodically after, since the dose is constrained below a threshold
of kidney function and the agent is avoided below a lower one. Gastrointestinal intolerance is the
common reason for stopping, and is reduced by gradual escalation and extended-release formulations.
Long-term use is associated with lower vitamin B12 levels, so periodic measurement is advised.

## Where cardiovascular, kidney or heart-failure disease is present

Guidance describes agents with demonstrated organ benefit as preferred, whether or not metformin is
also used, and independent of whether the HbA1c is at goal:

- **Established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease** — a GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven
  cardiovascular benefit, or an SGLT-2 inhibitor with proven cardiovascular benefit.
- **Heart failure**, particularly with reduced ejection fraction — an SGLT-2 inhibitor.
- **Chronic kidney disease**, particularly with albuminuria — an SGLT-2 inhibitor, with a GLP-1
  receptor agonist as the alternative where an SGLT-2 inhibitor is not tolerated or not appropriate.

These are organ-protective therapies that also lower glucose, rather than later steps in a glucose
ladder. Class-specific cautions are part of the choice: SGLT-2 inhibitors carry a risk of genital
mycotic infection, volume depletion, and — uncommonly — ketoacidosis at near-normal glucose levels,
which is easy to miss precisely because the glucose is not high.

## Where weight is a major treatment goal

Agents with the greatest weight-reduction effect — the GLP-1 receptor agonists and dual incretin
agonists — are preferred where weight management is a primary objective, alongside structured
lifestyle intervention and, in selected people, metabolic surgery.

## Reassessment

The regimen is reviewed every three to six months. De-intensification is an explicit option, not a
failure: where the regimen has become more aggressive than the individualized target requires, or
where low blood sugar is occurring, stepping down is the appropriate response.

Doses, titration schedules and prescription decisions are outside the scope of this note and of this
system.
