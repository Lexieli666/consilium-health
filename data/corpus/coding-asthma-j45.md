## The category and its two axes

Asthma is J45 in ICD-10-CM, in Chapter 10, diseases of the respiratory system. The category is never
used at three characters, and what fills the remaining ones is two independent axes crossed with
each other.

The first axis is severity, expressed in the classification's own vocabulary rather than the
patient's: mild intermittent, mild persistent, moderate persistent and severe persistent, plus an
"other and unspecified" branch for asthma documented without a severity level. The second axis is
the state of the disease at this encounter: uncomplicated, with acute exacerbation, or with status
asthmaticus.

Every combination of the two exists as a code. That is the whole shape of the category, and it is
why asthma questions so often reduce to two lookups rather than one.

## Exacerbation and status asthmaticus are exclusive

The two are alternatives, not a scale that can be climbed within a single code. Status asthmaticus
describes an attack that has not responded to treatment, and where it is documented the code says
status asthmaticus and does not also say acute exacerbation. Choosing between them is a
documentation question, not a clinical judgment the coder makes.

## The unspecified branch, and what it costs

Asthma documented with no severity stated is coded to the unspecified branch, which remains valid
and is frequently the only supportable choice. The classification does not permit severity to be
inferred from the treatment recorded, from a peak-flow value, or from how the condition is described
elsewhere in the chart. Where severity is not documented, unspecified is the correct code rather
than a fallback.

## Named variants inside the category

Two variants sit at the far end of the category with their own codes rather than in the severity
grid: exercise-induced bronchospasm and cough variant asthma. Occupational asthma attributed to an
identified agent is classified with the lung diseases due to external agents instead of here, and
the agent is named by an external-cause code.

## Asthma alongside chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

Where both are documented, the chronic obstructive pulmonary disease category carries an instruction
to add a code from J45 identifying the type of asthma, so the two are reported together rather than
one being chosen over the other. The reverse is not true: an asthma code alone does not imply the
other condition.

## Codes that commonly accompany J45

Tobacco is the standard companion. The category carries an explicit instruction to add a code
identifying tobacco dependence, use, exposure or a past history of use, and those live in the mental
and behavioral chapter and the factors-influencing-health chapter respectively. Allergic rhinitis
has its own category in the same chapter and is reported alongside rather than folded in. Wheeze,
cough and shortness of breath are symptoms and are not coded when asthma is documented, because they
are intrinsic to it.
