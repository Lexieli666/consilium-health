## The range and what it holds

Chapter 14 of ICD-10-CM covers N00 through N99: diseases of the urinary tract and of the male and
female genital organs, together with the breast. Every code begins with the letter N. The chapter
runs from the kidney outward along the urinary tract, then turns to the genital organs.

## The blocks, in order

Glomerular diseases open the chapter at N00 to N08, organized by clinical syndrome — acute and
chronic nephritic, rapidly progressive, recurrent hematuria, nephrotic — with the underlying
morphology carried in a shared set of subdivisions.

Renal tubulo-interstitial diseases follow at N10 to N16 and include acute and chronic
pyelonephritis. Acute kidney failure and chronic kidney disease occupy N17 to N19, and the chronic
category is subdivided by stage, which is the detail most other chapters reach in to use.

Urolithiasis sits at N20 to N23, other disorders of the kidney and ureter at N25 to N29.

Other diseases of the urinary system occupy N30 to N39 and hold most everyday urinary questions:
cystitis subdivided by acuteness and by whether hematuria is present, bladder-neck and urethral
disorders, urethritis, incontinence, and the category used for urinary tract infection when the site
is not specified.

The genital blocks follow. Diseases of male genital organs occupy N40 to N53, including prostatic
hyperplasia with its subdivision by lower urinary tract symptoms. Disorders of the breast sit at N60
to N65. Inflammatory diseases of the female pelvic organs occupy N70 to N77, and noninflammatory
disorders of the female genital tract N80 to N98, covering endometriosis, prolapse, menstrual
disorders, menopausal symptoms and infertility. The chapter closes at N99 with postprocedural
complications.

## What the chapter does not hold, and where those codes live

Sexually transmitted infections are classified in the infectious-disease chapter, so an infection
named by organism leaves this chapter even when the site is genitourinary. Urinary and genital
conditions complicating pregnancy, childbirth or the period after delivery belong to the obstetric
chapter, which takes precedence over the codes here. Sepsis is an infectious-disease code, and
"urosepsis" is not a codable term in ICD-10-CM at all — the record has to say which of the two is
meant. Neoplasms of any genitourinary organ are neoplasms; congenital malformations belong to the
congenital chapter.

Symptoms are the recurring boundary. Painful urination, urinary frequency and urgency, blood in the
urine reported as a finding, retention, and an abnormal urinalysis with no diagnosis recorded are
all classified in Chapter 18 rather than here.

## Codes from other chapters that genitourinary questions commonly need

Chronic kidney disease is the most-referenced block in this chapter from outside it: the hypertensive
and diabetic categories in other chapters pair with a stage code from N18. Diabetes supplies its own
kidney manifestation codes from the endocrine chapter. Long-term drug therapy, dialysis status,
transplant status, screening encounters and contraceptive management all come from the
factors-influencing-health chapter.
