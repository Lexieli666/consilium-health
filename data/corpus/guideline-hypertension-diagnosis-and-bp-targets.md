> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## What this guidance covers

How adult office blood pressure is classified, what is required before a diagnosis of hypertension
is made, and the treatment target most bodies set once treatment has begun.

## Classification of office blood pressure in adults

United States society guidance classifies adult office readings as:

- **Normal** — systolic below 120 mm Hg *and* diastolic below 80 mm Hg.
- **Elevated** — systolic 120 to 129 mm Hg *and* diastolic below 80 mm Hg.
- **Stage 1 hypertension** — systolic 130 to 139 mm Hg *or* diastolic 80 to 89 mm Hg.
- **Stage 2 hypertension** — systolic at least 140 mm Hg *or* diastolic at least 90 mm Hg.

When the systolic and diastolic values fall into different categories, the higher category applies.
"Elevated" is not a stage of hypertension: it marks a group at raised future risk in whom lifestyle
measures are advised and drug treatment is not.

## Measurement technique, which changes the number

A reading taken carelessly is not a milder version of a correct reading — it is a different
measurement. The technique that the classification above assumes is a validated device, a cuff
sized to the arm, the person seated with back supported and feet flat, arm supported at heart
level, no caffeine, exercise or smoking in the preceding 30 minutes, five minutes of rest
beforehand, and no talking during the measurement. An undersized cuff and an unsupported arm both
push the reading up, each by an amount comparable to the width of a whole stage.

## Confirming the diagnosis

A diagnosis is not made from a single reading. The standard is an average of two or more readings
taken on two or more separate occasions. Out-of-office measurement — home readings or 24-hour
ambulatory monitoring — confirms the diagnosis and identifies two patterns that office readings
alone cannot: white-coat hypertension, where office readings are raised but out-of-office readings
are not, and masked hypertension, where the reverse holds and the risk is underestimated.

## Treatment target

For most treated adults, United States guidance describes a target below 130/80 mm Hg. The target is
individualized for older adults with frailty, limited life expectancy, or a heavy burden of other
conditions, where the balance of benefit and harm differs and where postural drops matter more.

## Where guidance differs

The 130/80 mm Hg diagnostic threshold is a United States position. European guidance describes office
hypertension as beginning at 140/90 mm Hg, and treats the 130 to 139 / 80 to 89 band as high-normal
blood pressure rather than as stage 1 disease. The same reading is therefore labeled differently
depending on which body's guidance is applied, and the size of the population called hypertensive
changes substantially between the two. Any answer that quotes a threshold should say which
convention it is using.

## Follow-up interval

After treatment starts or changes, reassessment at roughly monthly intervals until the target is
reached, then review every three to six months once readings are stable.
