## The category and its two axes

Heart failure is I50 in ICD-10-CM, at the end of the "other forms of heart disease" block in Chapter
9. Like asthma, it is built from two axes crossed with each other, and unlike asthma both of them
depend entirely on what the record says.

The first axis is mechanism: systolic, diastolic, or combined systolic and diastolic. The second is
acuity: acute, chronic, or acute on chronic, with an unspecified option at each level. The category
also holds left ventricular failure, a set of other specified forms including right heart failure,
biventricular failure, high-output failure and end-stage heart failure, and an unspecified code for
heart failure documented with neither axis.

## The vocabulary problem

Records increasingly describe heart failure by ejection fraction rather than by mechanism — reduced,
preserved, mildly reduced, improved. The classification's terms are the older ones, and the mapping
between the two vocabularies is what most heart-failure coding questions are really about: reduced
ejection fraction corresponds to the systolic branch and preserved ejection fraction to the
diastolic branch. Where the record gives only an ejection fraction number and no descriptive term,
the classification does not permit the mechanism to be derived from it.

"Acute on chronic" requires both to be documented. A chronic condition with a current
decompensation is not automatically coded to that branch unless the record supports both parts.

## Congestive heart failure

"Congestive" is not a fourth branch. The term is inclusive across the category rather than
descriptive of a mechanism, so congestive heart failure with no further detail is coded to the
unspecified code, and congestive heart failure documented as systolic is coded to the systolic
branch. The word does not add specificity even though it reads as though it should.

## Sequencing with hypertensive heart disease

Where the record documents heart disease as hypertensive or as due to hypertension, the combination
code for hypertensive heart disease is reported first and a code from this category is added to
identify the type of heart failure. The hypertension coding note owns which combination code
applies; the point here is that the heart failure code is added rather than replaced.

## What is not coded here

Fluid overload, peripheral edema and pulmonary congestion documented as findings, without a heart
failure diagnosis, are classified with the symptoms and abnormal findings in Chapter 18. Heart
failure following cardiac surgery or a procedure has its own category among the postprocedural
complications. Rheumatic heart failure is classified with the rheumatic block at the start of the
chapter.

## Codes that commonly accompany I50

Chronic kidney disease from the genitourinary chapter and diabetes from the endocrine chapter appear
alongside constantly. Long-term drug therapy, body mass index, and the presence of a cardiac device
are status codes from the factors-influencing-health chapter, and tobacco use or exposure is
requested by categories throughout this chapter.
