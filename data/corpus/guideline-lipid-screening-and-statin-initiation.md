## Screening

The screening test is a lipid panel — total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol and
triglycerides. A non-fasting sample is acceptable for initial screening in most adults; a fasting
sample is preferred where triglycerides are markedly raised, since the triglyceride value and the
calculated LDL derived from it are the parts most affected by a recent meal.

The usual screening interval is every four to six years for adults aged 20 to 75 who are at low risk
and not on lipid-lowering therapy, and shorter where risk factors are present or where risk is being
reassessed before a treatment decision.

## Risk stratification

The central move in current guidance is that the decision is driven by estimated 10-year
atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk, not by the LDL number alone. Risk is estimated from age, sex,
race, total and HDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, treatment for hypertension, diabetes and
smoking status. The four named risk categories, for adults aged 40 to 75 without established
disease, are:

- **Low** — estimated 10-year risk below 5 percent.
- **Borderline** — 5 percent to below 7.5 percent.
- **Intermediate** — 7.5 percent to below 20 percent.
- **High** — 20 percent or above.

Risk-enhancing features shift a decision within the borderline and intermediate bands rather than
replacing the estimate: family history of premature cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease,
chronic inflammatory conditions, persistently raised triglycerides, metabolic syndrome, conditions
specific to pregnancy such as pre-eclampsia, and, where the decision remains uncertain, a coronary
artery calcium score.

## Groups in which therapy is indicated

- **Established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease** — high-intensity statin therapy as
  secondary prevention.
- **LDL cholesterol at or above 190 mg/dL** — high-intensity statin therapy regardless of the
  calculated risk score, which is not designed for this group.
- **Diabetes, aged 40 to 75** — at least moderate-intensity statin therapy.
- **Intermediate risk** — moderate-intensity statin therapy after a risk discussion.
- **Borderline risk** — guidance describes a risk discussion rather than a default action, with
  therapy favored where risk-enhancing features are present and reasonably deferred where they are
  not.

## Monitoring interval

The lipid panel is repeated four to twelve weeks after starting therapy or changing intensity, which
assesses response and adherence together, then every three to twelve months once stable. Routine
liver enzyme monitoring is not part of ongoing care in the absence of symptoms; a baseline
measurement is usual.

## Where guidance differs

Bodies differ on whether to state numeric LDL targets. United States guidance describes statin
intensity and percentage reduction from baseline; European guidance describes explicit LDL goals
that fall as risk category rises, pursued with combination therapy where a statin alone falls short. Answers should say
which framing they are using rather than blending the two, since "at goal" means different things
under each.
