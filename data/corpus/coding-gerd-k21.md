## The category

Gastroesophageal reflux disease (gastro-oesophageal in British spelling) is K21 in ICD-10-CM, in the
esophagus, stomach and duodenum block of Chapter 11. The category is short, and the single
distinction it draws is whether esophagitis is present.

Reflux disease with esophagitis is one branch, further divided by whether bleeding is documented;
reflux disease without esophagitis is the other. Both are complete codes, and the second is the one
used for the large majority of records, because the distinction rests on an endoscopic finding that
most records do not contain.

## What decides the branch

Esophagitis is a mucosal finding, not a symptom severity. The classification takes it from
documentation of the finding, and does not permit it to be inferred from how bad the heartburn is,
from the treatment prescribed, or from the duration of the condition. A record describing severe
reflux symptoms with no endoscopy is coded to the without-esophagitis branch.

Bleeding, where it subdivides the esophagitis branch, likewise has to be documented rather than
assumed from an anemia elsewhere in the chart.

## Neighboring categories that are not this one

Esophagitis without reflux disease has its own category immediately before this one, and reflux
disease is not assumed from it. Barrett esophagus is a separate category further along the block,
subdivided by whether dysplasia is present and by its grade; it is reported in addition to the
reflux code rather than instead of it, because the two are different conditions and one does not
include the other.

Hiatal hernia is a hernia and is coded from the hernia block, so a record documenting both a hiatal
hernia and reflux disease carries two codes from two blocks. Esophageal stricture, ulcer and
dysmotility are likewise separate categories.

Newborn reflux is excluded from this category outright and is classified in the perinatal chapter.

## Symptoms, and when they are coded instead

Heartburn, chest discomfort of esophageal origin, regurgitation, dysphagia and nausea are symptoms.
Where reflux disease is documented, they are intrinsic to it and are not separately coded. Where it
is not — where the record says heartburn and stops — the symptom code from Chapter 18 is what the
encounter supports, and reaching for the disease code instead is the error the boundary exists to
prevent. Dysphagia is the exception worth knowing: it is not routinely part of uncomplicated reflux
disease, and where it is documented it is reported in addition.

## Codes that commonly accompany K21

Long-term drug therapy status is coded from the factors-influencing-health chapter, as is body mass
index. Tobacco and alcohol use are coded from their own chapters. Where reflux disease is documented
alongside asthma or chronic cough, those are coded from the respiratory chapter and neither implies
the other.
