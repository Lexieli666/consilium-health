> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Lifestyle measures are the shared foundation

Every major body recommends the same set of non-drug measures, whether or not medication is also
used: reducing dietary sodium, adopting a dietary pattern rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains
and low-fat dairy and low in saturated fat, regular aerobic activity most days of the week, weight
reduction where body weight is above the healthy range, limiting alcohol, and stopping smoking.
Each produces a meaningful reduction on its own, and the effects are partly additive, so several
combined can move a person a full stage.

## When drug treatment starts

The decision is tied to cardiovascular risk, not to the blood-pressure number alone:

- **Stage 2 hypertension** — drug treatment alongside lifestyle measures, usually beginning with
  two agents, because a single agent rarely closes a gap of this size.
- **Stage 1 hypertension with established cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic kidney
  disease, or an estimated 10-year cardiovascular risk at or above 10 percent** — drug treatment
  alongside lifestyle measures.
- **Stage 1 hypertension without those features** — lifestyle measures first, with reassessment
  after three to six months before drug treatment is considered.

## First-line drug classes

Four classes are first-line for most adults without a compelling indication for something else:
thiazide-type diuretics, angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers,
and dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers. An ACE inhibitor and an angiotensin receptor blocker
are not combined — the combination adds harm without adding benefit. Single-pill combinations are
preferred where two agents are needed, because adherence falls as pill count rises.

Comorbidity shifts the choice. Renin-angiotensin blockade is preferred where diabetes with
albuminuria or chronic kidney disease is present; a beta-blocker is not first-line for uncomplicated
hypertension but becomes appropriate where there is a separate indication such as heart failure or
recent myocardial infarction. Doses are outside the scope of this note and of this system.

## Resistant hypertension

Blood pressure above target on three agents including a diuretic, all at appropriate doses, is
described as resistant hypertension. Before treatment is escalated further, the sequence is to
confirm the readings out of office, review adherence, review agents that raise blood pressure —
non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, decongestants, some stimulants, combined oral contraceptives
— and investigate for a secondary cause. Apparent resistance is more often measurement, adherence
or an interfering agent than a need for a fourth drug.

## Where guidance differs

Bodies differ on how aggressively to treat low-risk stage 1 hypertension, which follows from the
threshold disagreement in the diagnostic note: where guidance describes hypertension as beginning at
140/90 mm Hg, much of the population that United States guidance calls stage 1 is instead advised on
lifestyle alone.

## Follow-up interval

Monthly review until the target is reached, including adherence and a check of kidney function and
potassium after starting an agent acting on the renin-angiotensin system, then review every three
to six months once stable.
