## The category

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is J44 in ICD-10-CM, in Chapter 10, among the chronic lower
respiratory diseases. Unlike the asthma category beside it, J44 has no severity axis. What its
fourth character records is whether a complication is present at this encounter.

Three subdivisions carry most of the work: the disease with an acute lower respiratory infection,
the disease with an acute exacerbation, and the disease without either, which is the unspecified
code used for a stable presentation. The category also carries subdivisions for other specified
forms.

## The two complication codes are not alternatives

This is the detail that distinguishes J44 from most complication-bearing categories. An infection
and an exacerbation are different events, and someone can have both at once. Where both are
documented, both codes are reported — the classification does not treat the pair as mutually
exclusive the way asthma treats exacerbation and status asthmaticus.

The infection code also does not name the infection. It records that one is present, and a second
code identifying the organism or the specific infection is reported in addition, from whichever
category owns it. Pneumonia accompanying the disease is coded that way rather than being absorbed.

## What the category includes and what sits beside it

The category takes in the overlapping descriptions that clinical records use for the same disease —
chronic obstructive bronchitis, chronic bronchitis with emphysema, chronic obstructive asthma.
Emphysema without chronic bronchitis has its own category, as do simple and mucopurulent chronic
bronchitis and bronchitis not specified as acute or chronic; those are neighbors in the same block,
not subdivisions of this one.

Where asthma is documented alongside, this category carries an instruction to add a code identifying
the type of asthma, so both appear.

## Respiratory failure, and sequencing

Acute or chronic respiratory failure is a separate category at the end of the chapter and is
reported in addition when documented. Which of the two is sequenced first depends on the
circumstances of the encounter rather than on a fixed rule, and it is a sequencing question the
record has to answer.

## Codes that commonly accompany J44

The category carries an explicit instruction to add a code identifying tobacco dependence, use,
exposure or a past history of use, and in practice one of those accompanies most records here.
Occupational or environmental exposure is coded from the factors-influencing-health chapter, with
the external-cause chapter naming the agent. Long-term oxygen use and long-term drug therapy are
status codes. Shortness of breath, cough and sputum production are symptoms intrinsic to the disease
and are not coded separately once it is documented.
