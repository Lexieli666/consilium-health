## The range and what it holds

Chapter 11 of ICD-10-CM covers K00 through K95 and follows the digestive tract in anatomical order
from the mouth to the anus, then adds the organs that drain into it. Every code begins with the
letter K.

## The blocks, in order

Diseases of the oral cavity and salivary glands open the chapter at K00 to K14, covering the teeth
and their supporting structures, the jaws, the salivary glands, the tongue and the lips.

Diseases of the esophagus (oesophagus), stomach and duodenum occupy K20 to K31. Esophagitis comes
first, then gastroesophageal reflux disease, then the Barrett and other esophageal categories,
followed by the peptic ulcer categories divided by site — gastric, duodenal, peptic unspecified and
gastrojejunal — and then gastritis, duodenitis and the functional stomach disorders.

Diseases of the appendix follow at K35 to K38 and hernia at K40 to K46, where the block is organized
first by site and then by whether obstruction or gangrene is present.

Noninfective enteritis and colitis occupy K50 to K52, which is where Crohn disease and ulcerative
colitis are classified. Other diseases of the intestines follow at K55 to K64: vascular disorders of
the bowel, ileus and obstruction, diverticular disease, irritable bowel syndrome, constipation, anal
and rectal conditions, and the categories for gastrointestinal bleeding when no more specific source
is documented. Diseases of the peritoneum and retroperitoneum sit at K65 to K68.

Diseases of the liver occupy K70 to K77, ordered from alcoholic and toxic liver disease through
hepatic failure, chronic hepatitis and fibrosis to cirrhosis. Disorders of the gallbladder, biliary
tract and pancreas follow at K80 to K87, and the chapter closes at K90 to K95 with malabsorption,
postprocedural digestive disorders and the residual categories.

## What the chapter does not hold, and where those codes live

Infectious gastroenteritis and colitis are classified in the infectious-disease chapter, not here,
and the distinction turns on whether an organism is documented. Viral hepatitis is likewise an
infection rather than a liver disease for coding purposes, though its chronic sequelae may be coded
from this chapter in addition. Neoplasms of any digestive organ are neoplasms. Hernia complicating
pregnancy and digestive disorders arising in pregnancy belong to the obstetric chapter.

Symptoms are the boundary that recurs. Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, heartburn, dysphagia,
change in bowel habit and an abnormal liver function test with no diagnosis recorded are classified
in Chapter 18. A few bleeding presentations are exceptions: hematemesis, melena and unspecified
gastrointestinal hemorrhage have categories inside this chapter rather than with the symptoms.

## Codes from other chapters that digestive questions commonly need

Alcohol use, abuse and dependence are coded from the mental and behavioral chapter and are commonly
reported alongside the liver categories here. Adverse effects of drugs come from the injury and
poisoning chapter. Body mass index, long-term drug therapy, family history of digestive disease and
screening encounters such as colorectal cancer screening all come from the factors-influencing-health
chapter.
