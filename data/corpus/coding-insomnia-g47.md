## Two categories in two chapters

Insomnia is the clearest example in ICD-10-CM of one complaint classified in two different chapters,
and choosing between them is the whole of the problem.

G47 sits in the nervous system chapter, in the episodic and paroxysmal block, and holds the sleep
disorders of organic origin. Its insomnia subdivisions cover insomnia unspecified, insomnia due to a
medical condition, and other specified insomnia.

F51 sits in the mental and behavioral chapter, in the block for behavioral syndromes associated with
physiological disturbances, and holds sleep disorders *not* due to a substance or a known
physiological condition. Its insomnia subdivisions are the ones a sleep clinic vocabulary uses:
primary insomnia, adjustment insomnia, paradoxical insomnia, psychophysiologic insomnia, insomnia
due to another mental disorder, and other specified.

## What decides which chapter

The dividing question is whether the record attributes the insomnia to a physiological cause. Where
it does — a medical condition named as responsible — the code comes from the nervous system chapter,
and a second code identifying that condition is reported with it. Where the record attributes it to
a mental disorder, or describes it in the primary, adjustment or psychophysiologic terms, the code
comes from the mental and behavioral chapter.

Where the record says only "insomnia", with no attribution at all, the classification defaults to
the unspecified code in the nervous system chapter. That default is worth knowing precisely because
it is not intuitive: the unqualified term does not land in the psychological branch.

## The rest of the G47 category

The category holds more than insomnia, and questions about sleep frequently arrive at one of the
others. Hypersomnia, circadian rhythm sleep disorders, the sleep apnea (apnoea) subdivisions
including the obstructive form, narcolepsy, the parasomnias including sleepwalking and night
terrors, and sleep-related movement disorders are all in the same category or immediately beside it
in the same chapter. Restless legs syndrome sits in the movement disorder block earlier in the
chapter rather than with the sleep codes.

Obstructive sleep apnea being here rather than in the respiratory chapter is the misplacement people
make most often in this area.

## Symptoms, and what is not a diagnosis

Somnolence, drowsiness and a complaint of poor sleep with no diagnosis recorded are classified with
the symptoms in Chapter 18. Sleep deprivation — insufficient sleep because of circumstances rather
than an inability to sleep — is not a disorder in this category at all; it is a
factors-influencing-health code, and the distinction between not sleeping and not having the
opportunity to sleep is one the classification takes seriously.

## Codes that commonly accompany a sleep code

Where insomnia is attributed to a medical or mental condition, that condition's code is reported
with it, and the anxiety and depressive categories are the most frequent companions. Long-term drug
therapy status and body mass index come from the factors-influencing-health chapter, the latter
alongside the sleep apnea codes in particular.
