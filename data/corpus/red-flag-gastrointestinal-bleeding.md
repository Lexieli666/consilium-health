> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Call emergency services immediately

- Vomiting blood, or vomit that looks like coffee grounds.
- Black, tarry, foul-smelling stool — melena, which indicates digested blood from the upper
  gastrointestinal tract.
- Large-volume or continuing rectal bleeding.
- Any gastrointestinal bleeding with dizziness, faintness, collapse, palpitations, cold clammy
  skin, confusion, or breathlessness — these indicate significant blood loss regardless of how much
  blood has been seen.
- Bleeding in someone taking an anticoagulant or with known liver disease or esophageal varices.

## Seek same-day medical assessment

Smaller amounts of fresh rectal bleeding without the features above, particularly when new,
persistent, or accompanied by a change in bowel habit, unexplained weight loss, or iron-deficiency
anemia (anaemia). Bleeding attributed to hemorrhoids still warrants assessment when it is new or
changed, because the presence of hemorrhoids does not exclude another cause higher up.

## Why black stool matters as much as visible blood

Blood from the stomach or duodenum is digested during transit and arrives black and tarry rather
than red. Melena therefore indicates bleeding that has been ongoing long enough to transit, and it
frequently accompanies a larger volume of loss than the appearance suggests. It is easy to dismiss
because it does not look like blood.

Iron supplements and bismuth-containing medications also darken the stool. That resemblance is a
common source of false reassurance in the wrong direction — the safer assumption is bleeding until
assessment says otherwise.

## Why the symptoms of blood loss matter more than the volume seen

People routinely underestimate how much blood they have lost, and a significant proportion of the
loss may not be visible. Dizziness on standing, a fast pulse, pallor, sweating and breathlessness
indicate that circulating volume is falling, and they can appear before any dramatic quantity of
blood is observed. They are the more reliable signal.

## What not to do

Do not eat or drink while waiting, in case an urgent endoscopy or an operation is needed. Do not
stop or continue an anticoagulant on your own judgment — bring the medication list, and let the
treating team make that decision. Do not treat presumed hemorrhoidal bleeding at home when any of
the emergency features above are present.

## While waiting

Keep the person lying down with legs raised if faint, keep them warm, and gather their medication
list, particularly anticoagulants, antiplatelets and anti-inflammatory drugs. Note the color and
approximate volume of what was passed or vomited, which genuinely helps the receiving team.
