## The range and what it holds

Chapter 5 of ICD-10-CM covers F01 through F99: mental, behavioral and neurodevelopmental disorders.
Every code begins with the letter F, and the letter is used for nothing else, so an F code is always
in this chapter.

The chapter is ordered roughly by presumed origin — organic causes first, then substance-related,
then the disorders grouped by presentation, then those with onset in childhood.

## The blocks, in order

Mental disorders due to a known physiological condition open the chapter at F01 to F09, covering the
dementias and the cognitive and personality changes attributed to brain disease or injury. Mental
and behavioral disorders due to psychoactive substance use follow at F10 to F19, one category per
substance class, each subdivided by whether the pattern is use, abuse or dependence and by the
complication present.

Schizophrenia, schizotypal, delusional and other non-mood psychotic disorders occupy F20 to F29.
Mood disorders follow at F30 to F39, running from the manic and bipolar categories through the
depressive ones, which are subdivided by single versus recurrent episode and by severity.

Anxiety, dissociative, stress-related, somatoform and other non-psychotic mental disorders make up
F40 to F48 — the phobias, panic, generalized anxiety disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder,
reactions to severe stress including post-traumatic stress disorder, and the somatic symptom
categories.

Behavioral syndromes associated with physiological disturbances and physical factors sit at F50 to
F59, which is where the eating disorders are and where sleep disorders *not* attributed to a
substance or a known physiological condition are classified. Disorders of adult personality and
behavior follow at F60 to F69.

The developmental and childhood-onset blocks close the chapter: intellectual disabilities at F70 to
F79, pervasive and specific developmental disorders at F80 to F89, and behavioral and emotional
disorders with onset in childhood or adolescence at F90 to F98, with F99 as the unspecified
residual.

## What the chapter does not hold, and where those codes live

Sleep disorders of organic origin are classified with the nervous system rather than here, and the
split between the two chapters is the whole difficulty of coding insomnia. Symptoms and signs
involving emotional state and behavior — including suicidal ideation, nervousness, restlessness and
an abnormal cognitive screening result with no diagnosis attached — are classified in Chapter 18,
not in this chapter. A personal history of self-harm is a status code from the
factors-influencing-health chapter.

## Codes from other chapters that mental-health questions commonly need

Substance-related categories here interlock with poisoning and adverse-effect codes from Chapter 19,
and the sequencing depends on whether the substance was taken as prescribed. Tobacco dependence is
an F code, while tobacco exposure and a past history of tobacco use are Z codes; circulatory and
respiratory categories that call for a tobacco code may be satisfied from either chapter. Social
determinants — housing, employment, relationship and support circumstances — are Z codes and are
reported alongside a diagnosis rather than as one.
