> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Get help now

Thoughts of suicide or of harming oneself are a reason to reach a person immediately, not to wait
for a routine appointment.

- **Immediate danger** — an attempt has been made, or the means are at hand and the intent is
  present: call emergency services.
- **Thoughts of suicide without immediate danger** — contact a crisis line, an urgent mental health
  service, or a physician the same day. In the United States, the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline is
  reachable by call or text. Many countries operate an equivalent service; local numbers differ and
  are worth knowing in advance.
- Do not leave someone who is at immediate risk alone.

## What raises immediate concern

A specific plan, access to means, a stated intent, a recent attempt, putting affairs in order or
giving away possessions, an abrupt shift from severe distress to unexpected calm, severe
hopelessness, command hallucinations, and intoxication alongside any of these. Recent loss,
discharge from psychiatric care, and a previous attempt all raise risk.

The absence of these does not make the situation safe. Risk fluctuates, and the reliability of any
prediction about an individual is poor — which is why the response is based on the presence of the
thoughts, not on an assessment of how likely they are to be acted on.

## Points that are frequently misunderstood

**Asking about suicide does not plant the idea.** This is one of the most consistent findings in the
area, and the belief otherwise is a common reason people avoid the conversation with someone they
are worried about. Asking directly and calmly is helpful.

**Talking about it is not attention-seeking**, and is not to be treated as a reason to take it less
seriously. Most people who die by suicide communicated something beforehand.

**Improvement is not automatically reassurance.** A sudden lift in mood after a period of severe
depression sometimes follows a decision having been made, and it warrants more attention rather than
less.

## What helps in the moment

Stay with the person or stay on the line. Listen without arguing, minimizing, or rushing to solve.
Remove or secure access to means where that can be done safely — medications, firearms, sharp
objects — since restricting access measurably reduces deaths. Help them make the contact rather than
leaving them to do it alone.

## This system's position

This system is not a crisis service, and no answer it produces substitutes for speaking to a person.
Any input matching this note causes it to escalate immediately and to lead with how to reach help,
before anything else it might say. Where the input suggests immediate danger, the only appropriate
response is directing to emergency services.
