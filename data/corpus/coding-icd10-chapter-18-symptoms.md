> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## The range and what it holds

Chapter 18 of ICD-10-CM covers R00 through R99: symptoms, signs and abnormal clinical and laboratory
findings not elsewhere classified. Every code begins with the letter R. It is the chapter for the
state a record is in before a diagnosis exists, and almost every other chapter has a boundary with
it.

The rule that governs the whole chapter is about when *not* to use it. An R code is assigned when
the documentation records a symptom, a sign or an abnormal result and no more specific diagnosis has
been established. Once a diagnosis is documented, the diagnosis code replaces the symptom code —
with one exception, for a symptom that is not routinely associated with that diagnosis, which is
reported in addition because it carries information the diagnosis does not.

## The blocks, in order

The first blocks follow body systems. Symptoms and signs involving the circulatory and respiratory
systems open the chapter at R00 to R09 and hold some of the most-used codes in it: palpitations, an
elevated blood-pressure reading in someone with no diagnosis of hypertension, cough, breathing
abnormalities including shortness of breath, hemoptysis and chest pain. Digestive and abdominal
symptoms follow at R10 to R19 — abdominal pain by site, nausea and vomiting, heartburn, dysphagia,
flatulence, change in bowel habit and an abdominal mass.

Symptoms involving the skin sit at R20 to R23, and those involving the nervous and musculoskeletal
systems at R25 to R29, covering abnormal involuntary movements, gait and mobility abnormalities,
lack of coordination and other abnormal neurological findings. Genitourinary symptoms occupy R30 to
R39: painful urination, urinary frequency and urgency, hematuria reported as a finding, retention
and incontinence where no diagnosis is recorded.

Symptoms involving cognition, perception, emotional state and behavior follow at R40 to R46. This is
where somnolence, coma, disorientation, memory loss and an abnormal cognitive screening result are
classified, and it is also where suicidal ideation is coded. Speech and voice disturbances sit at
R47 to R49.

General symptoms and signs occupy R50 to R69 — fever, headache, malaise and fatigue, syncope,
convulsions not identified as epilepsy, dizziness, shock, enlarged lymph nodes, volume depletion,
abnormal weight loss and pain not classified elsewhere.

The chapter then turns to results without a diagnosis: abnormal blood findings at R70 to R79,
abnormal urine findings at R80 to R82, abnormal findings in other body fluids and tissues at R83 to
R89, and abnormal diagnostic imaging and function studies at R90 to R94. Elevated glucose levels
including prediabetes sit inside the blood-findings block. Abnormal tumor markers are at R97, and
R99 closes the chapter with ill-defined and unknown cause of mortality.

## Codes from other chapters that symptom questions commonly need

Every diagnostic chapter is the destination for these codes once a diagnosis exists, so the useful
direction of travel is outward: chest pain to the circulatory chapter, cough and breathlessness to
the respiratory chapter, abdominal pain to the digestive chapter, painful urination to the
genitourinary chapter, prediabetes to the endocrine chapter once diabetes is diagnosed. Encounters
for observation or examination where no condition is found are not R codes at all — they belong to
the factors-influencing-health chapter.
