> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Acute treatment

An attack is treated early, while pain is still mild, because response rates fall once an attack is
established and central sensitization has set in. The options are grouped by evidence strength
rather than presented as a ranked list for an individual:

- Simple analgesics and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs for mild to moderate attacks.
- Triptans for moderate to severe attacks, or where simple analgesics have failed.
- The gepant and ditan classes where triptans are ineffective, not tolerated, or contraindicated by
  cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease — which is the main limitation on triptan use.
- Anti-nausea agents as adjuncts where nausea limits oral treatment; non-oral routes where vomiting
  or gastric stasis makes an oral route unreliable.

Treating on two or three separate attacks before judging an agent ineffective is the usual standard,
since attack-to-attack variability is high.

## When preventive treatment starts

Preventive treatment is offered when attacks are frequent or disabling enough that acute treatment
alone is insufficient. Guidance describes the threshold as four or more migraine days per month with
some disability, or fewer attacks where disability is severe, where acute treatment is ineffective
or contraindicated, or where the person prefers it. The aim is a reduction in frequency and
severity, not the elimination of attacks: a 50 percent reduction in migraine days is the conventional
definition of a treatment response.

## Preventive classes

Classes with supporting evidence include beta-blockers, the antiepileptics topiramate and valproate,
the tricyclic amitriptyline, candesartan, and the CGRP-targeted monoclonal antibodies and oral
gepants. The choice is made on comorbidity, side-effect profile and pregnancy status rather than by
a fixed order — an agent that also treats a coexisting condition is preferred, and valproate and
topiramate require particular care in people who could become pregnant.

## Assessing response

An adequate trial precedes any judgment of failure: commonly eight to twelve weeks at an appropriate
dose. A headache diary is the instrument for that judgment, because recall of attack frequency is
unreliable and tends to be dominated by the worst recent attack rather than the average.

## Medication-overuse headache

This is the point guidance emphasizes most. Frequent use of acute medication can itself sustain a
headache disorder, converting an episodic pattern into a chronic one. The limits are fewer than ten
treatment days per month for triptans, ergots, opioids and combination analgesics, and fewer than
fifteen days per month for simple analgesics. Overuse is a common and reversible reason for
headaches having become chronic, and it is checked for before a preventive regimen is escalated.

## What is outside this note

Doses, titration and prescription decisions are outside the scope of this note and of this system.
Sudden severe headache reaching maximum intensity within a minute, headache with fever and neck
stiffness, headache with new neurological deficit, and headache after head injury are emergency
presentations covered elsewhere in this corpus, not migraine management questions.
