> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## The core recommendation

Every major body places non-pharmacologic management first, and this is not a courtesy placement
ahead of the real treatment. Structured exercise carries the strongest and most consistent evidence
of any intervention for knee and hip osteoarthritis, with effect sizes comparable to pharmacologic
options and a far better harm profile.

The described program combines muscle strengthening around the affected joint, range-of-movement
work, and aerobic activity, delivered with supervision at least initially, because adherence and
correct progression determine the result. Exercise is described as beneficial regardless of
radiographic severity, age, or degree of pain at the outset — the common belief that a badly worn
joint should be rested is contradicted by the evidence, and pain during exercise within tolerable
limits is not a sign of damage.

Weight management is described alongside it where body weight is above the healthy range, with
clinically meaningful improvement at reductions of five to ten percent and further gains beyond
that. The two together outperform either alone.

## Adjunctive non-drug measures

Education and self-management support, pacing, appropriate footwear, walking aids where they
improve function, and heat or cold for symptomatic relief. Braces and orthoses are described for
selected patterns. Acupuncture, ultrasound, and similar modalities are described with weak or
conflicting evidence, and guidance differs on whether to mention them at all.

## Pharmacologic options

Described as adjuncts to the exercise program rather than as substitutes for it:

- **Topical non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs** — preferred first pharmacologic option for knee
  and hand osteoarthritis, with lower systemic exposure than oral agents.
- **Oral non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs** — effective, at the lowest effective dose for the
  shortest period, with gastrointestinal, cardiovascular and kidney risk assessed first.
- **Intra-articular corticosteroid injection** — short-term relief for a flare, with the effect
  measured in weeks.
- **Duloxetine** — described for people with widespread pain or inadequate response to the above.

Opioids are described as not recommended for osteoarthritis, on evidence of limited benefit and
substantial harm. Glucosamine and chondroitin are described as not recommended by most bodies, on
evidence of no clinically meaningful effect. Doses are outside the scope of this system.

## Surgery

Joint replacement is described for people with persistent pain and functional limitation despite
adequate non-surgical management, and it is highly effective in that group. Arthroscopic surgery for
degenerative knee disease is described as not recommended, on evidence showing no benefit over
placebo procedures.

## Where guidance differs

Intra-articular corticosteroid injection is a live disagreement. Some guidance describes it as a
conditional recommendation for short-term relief; other guidance is more cautious, citing modest
duration of benefit and evidence suggesting repeated injections may be associated with cartilage
loss. Bodies also differ on topical capsaicin and on whether platelet-rich plasma and similar
injectables should be mentioned as options or explicitly discouraged.
