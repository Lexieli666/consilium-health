> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Call emergency services immediately

- Breathlessness at rest, or coming on suddenly.
- Inability to speak in full sentences, or having to pause for breath between words.
- Blue or gray discoloration of the lips, tongue, face or fingertips.
- Drowsiness, confusion, agitation or exhaustion in someone struggling to breathe.
- Gasping, or noisy breathing with a high-pitched sound on breathing in.
- Chest pain with breathlessness.
- In an asthma attack: no improvement after reliever treatment, or symptoms returning within a few
  hours, or a chest that has gone quiet.
- In a child: fast breathing, the skin sucking in between or under the ribs, grunting, flaring of
  the nostrils, or a child too breathless to feed or talk.

## The signs that are most often misread

**Drowsiness in someone working hard to breathe is a late and serious sign, not a sign of settling.**
It can reflect rising carbon dioxide or exhaustion of the muscles of breathing, and it frequently
precedes respiratory arrest. Calm in this setting is not improvement.

**A quiet chest during a severe asthma attack is dangerous, not reassuring.** Wheeze is produced by
air moving through narrowed airways; when too little air moves to make the sound, the obstruction
has worsened rather than resolved.

**Normal oxygen saturation does not exclude a serious problem.** Saturation can be maintained until
compensation fails, particularly in younger people, and a home device gives an incomplete picture at
best. Work of breathing and the ability to speak are more informative than a number from a
fingertip device.

## Why the threshold is set low

The causes of acute severe breathlessness include asthma attack, COPD exacerbation, pneumonia,
pulmonary embolism, heart failure with fluid in the lungs, anaphylaxis, pneumothorax, and airway
obstruction. Several are immediately life-threatening, several are treatable if reached in time, and
they are not reliably distinguishable without assessment. That combination is what makes escalation
on the symptom rather than the suspected cause the correct policy.

## While waiting

Keep the person sitting upright, which is usually the easiest position; loosen tight clothing; keep
the room calm, since anxiety increases the work of breathing. Someone with prescribed reliever
inhaler or emergency medication uses it per their own action plan while help is on the way. If
anaphylaxis is possible, the anaphylaxis note in this corpus takes precedence and adrenaline comes
first. Note when the breathlessness began and what preceded it, which helps the receiving team
narrow the cause quickly.

This system does not diagnose. Any presentation matching this note is escalated, and no answer it
produces is a substitute for emergency assessment.
