> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Call emergency services immediately

- A headache that comes on abruptly and reaches maximum intensity within about a minute — often
  described as the worst headache of one's life, or as being hit on the head. This is a thunderclap
  headache.
- Headache with fever and a stiff neck, or with a rash that does not fade under pressure.
- Headache with new confusion, drowsiness, seizure, weakness, numbness, difficulty speaking, or
  visual loss.
- Headache after a head injury, particularly with vomiting, drowsiness or worsening over hours.
- Headache with sudden severe eye pain, a red eye, and blurred vision or halos around lights.

## Seek same-day medical assessment

- A new type of headache beginning after age 50.
- Headache that steadily worsens over days or weeks, or that is consistently worse on waking, on
  lying flat, or on coughing, straining or bending.
- New headache in someone with cancer, HIV, immunosuppression, or on anticoagulation.
- New headache in pregnancy or the postpartum period, particularly with visual changes, swelling or
  upper abdominal pain.
- Headache with scalp tenderness or jaw pain on chewing in someone over 50.

## Why thunderclap headache is treated as an emergency

The pattern is the concern, not the severity. A headache that reaches peak intensity almost
instantly can indicate bleeding around the brain from a ruptured aneurysm, and several other serious
causes present the same way. The mortality is high and depends on how quickly it is treated.

The critical point is that a thunderclap headache warrants emergency assessment **even if it then
settles completely**, including where simple painkillers appear to have relieved it. Resolution does
not exclude the diagnosis, and a warning bleed can precede a much larger one by days.

## Why the others are on the list

Fever with a stiff neck raises meningitis; a non-blanching rash raises meningococcal infection.
Headache worse on waking or on straining raises raised pressure inside the skull. Scalp tenderness
and jaw claudication over 50 raise giant cell arteritis, which can cause irreversible blindness
within days and is one of the few headache causes where hours of delay change the outcome
permanently. Eye pain with a red eye and halos raises acute angle-closure glaucoma, also
sight-threatening.

## What this note is not for

Most headaches are not dangerous. Migraine and tension-type headache are common, recurrent, and
recognizable by pattern, and they are covered by the migraine notes in this corpus. The purpose here
is to identify the minority of presentations where the pattern itself — sudden onset, new type,
progressive course, or accompanying neurological or systemic features — is the warning, independent
of how severe the pain feels.

This system does not diagnose. Any presentation matching this note is escalated, and no answer it
produces is a substitute for emergency assessment.
