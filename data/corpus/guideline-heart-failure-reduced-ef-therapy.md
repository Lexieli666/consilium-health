> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## The classification the guidance depends on

Heart failure guidance separates by ejection fraction, because the evidence differs sharply between
the groups:

- **Reduced ejection fraction** — 40 percent or below.
- **Mildly reduced ejection fraction** — 41 to 49 percent.
- **Preserved ejection fraction** — 50 percent or above.

The therapy below applies to the reduced group. Applying it wholesale to preserved ejection fraction
is a common error: several of these classes have not shown the same benefit there, and the one that
has — the SGLT-2 inhibitors — is the newest addition.

## The four foundational classes

Current guidance describes four classes as foundational, started together or in rapid sequence
rather than added one at a time over months:

1. **Renin-angiotensin system blockade** — an angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor where
   suitable, otherwise an ACE inhibitor, otherwise an angiotensin receptor blocker.
2. **A beta-blocker** with evidence specifically in heart failure, started when the person is not
   congested, since starting during congestion can worsen it.
3. **A mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist**, with potassium and kidney function monitored.
4. **An SGLT-2 inhibitor**, used for this indication irrespective of whether diabetes is present.

Each is titrated toward the doses used in the trials as blood pressure, kidney function and
potassium allow, with review at roughly two-week intervals during titration. The change from older
guidance is the emphasis on getting all four classes started at low intensity before maximizing any
one of them: the benefit of a fourth class at a low dose exceeds the benefit of pushing a third to
its ceiling. A modest early fall in kidney function or a small rise in potassium is anticipated and
is not by itself a reason to stop.

## Additional measures

Diuretic therapy relieves congestion and symptoms; it is not an outcome-modifying therapy and is
adjusted to the lowest dose that maintains euvolemia. Structured education in self-monitoring —
daily weights, recognizing increasing breathlessness or swelling, sodium and fluid advice — and
cardiac rehabilitation are part of the package. Iron deficiency is looked for and corrected, since
it is common and independently affects symptoms.

## Reassessment and device consideration

Ejection fraction is reassessed after at least three months of optimized therapy before an
implantable defibrillator or resynchronization device is considered, because function improves in a
substantial proportion of people once therapy is established. A device decision made on the
pre-treatment number is a decision made on the wrong number.

## Follow-up after admission

Early follow-up after a heart-failure admission — commonly within seven to fourteen days — is one of
the higher-yield contacts in the whole pathway, because the weeks after discharge carry the highest
readmission risk and are when titration most often stalls.
