> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Call emergency services immediately

Chest discomfort with any of the following is treated as a possible heart attack until proven
otherwise:

- Pressure, tightness, squeezing, heaviness or burning in the center or left of the chest, lasting
  more than a few minutes, or going away and coming back.
- Discomfort spreading to one or both arms, the shoulders, neck, jaw, back, or upper abdomen.
- Chest discomfort with breathlessness, cold sweat, nausea or vomiting, light-headedness, or a sense
  of impending doom.
- Chest discomfort at rest, or brought on by less exertion than usual in someone with known angina.
- Any chest discomfort in someone with known coronary artery disease, diabetes, or several
  cardiovascular risk factors.

Emergency services are called rather than arranging transport privately. Paramedics can record an
ECG, begin treatment on the way, and route to a hospital capable of intervention. Self-transport
loses all three, and it is dangerous if the person deteriorates during the journey.

## Why the threshold is set low

Time is the determining factor in the outcome of a heart attack: heart muscle dies progressively
from the moment the artery occludes, and treatment that restores flow early preserves function that
cannot be recovered later. The most common and most costly error is waiting — to see whether it
settles, to avoid embarrassment at a false alarm, or because the discomfort is mild.

A false alarm is a good outcome. This is the clearest case in medicine where over-escalation is the
correct policy, and it is why this system escalates on the possibility rather than the probability.

## Presentations that are missed

Not all heart attacks present with severe chest pain. Breathlessness alone, unusual fatigue,
indigestion-like discomfort, nausea, or discomfort felt mainly in the jaw, back or upper abdomen are
recognized presentations, and are relatively more common in women, in older adults, and in people
with diabetes, in whom nerve damage can blunt pain. The absence of classic pain is not reassuring.

Heartburn and cardiac pain are not reliably distinguishable by how they feel, and a history of
reflux does not exclude a cardiac cause. Antacid relief does not rule it out either.

## Other emergencies presenting as chest pain

Sudden tearing chest or back pain, sudden pleuritic chest pain with breathlessness, and chest pain
with collapse can indicate aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, or another immediately
life-threatening condition. All are emergencies, and none is distinguishable from cardiac pain
without assessment.

## While waiting

Stay with the person, keep them still and seated or resting, and be ready to describe when the
symptoms started. If the person becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally, emergency
dispatchers can guide chest compressions over the phone.

This system does not diagnose. Any presentation matching this note is escalated, and no answer it
produces is a substitute for emergency assessment.
