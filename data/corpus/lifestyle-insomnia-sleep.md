## What this note is and is not

These are the day-to-day habits that support sleep. They are supportive measures, not a treatment
for chronic insomnia in their own right — the insomnia guideline note in this corpus covers what the
first-line treatment is and why habit change alone does not substitute for it. What follows is the
practical content of the habits themselves.

## The wake time, not the bedtime

The single most useful anchor is a consistent wake time, held seven days a week. Sleep timing is
regulated by a body clock that takes its strongest cue from morning light, and a wake time that
moves by two or three hours at weekends shifts the clock in a way that makes falling asleep on a
weeknight harder. Bedtime is allowed to vary; the wake time is the part that is held.

Going to bed earlier to compensate for a bad night generally backfires, because time in bed spent
awake is what teaches the association between bed and wakefulness.

## Light

Bright light in the first hour or two after waking advances the clock and strengthens the signal
separating day from night. Outdoor light is far stronger than indoor lighting even on an overcast
day, so a short walk in the morning does more than a lamp. In the evening the aim is the reverse:
dimmer light in the last hour or two.

Screens matter less for their light than for what is on them. A device that is emotionally
activating at midnight is a problem whatever its color temperature.

## Caffeine, alcohol and nicotine

Caffeine has a long half-life, and a meaningful fraction of an afternoon dose is still present at
bedtime. People who report that coffee does not affect them are usually reporting that it does not
stop them falling asleep, which is a different thing from it not fragmenting the night. A cut-off in
the early afternoon is the common practical approach.

Alcohol shortens the time to fall asleep and then degrades the second half of the night, producing
lighter, more broken sleep and early waking. It is the most frequently self-prescribed sleep aid and
the one most likely to entrench the problem. Nicotine is a stimulant and withdrawal across the night
contributes to early waking.

## Naps

A short early-afternoon nap is compatible with good night sleep for most people. Long or late naps
reduce the sleep pressure that has been building since waking, and where insomnia is the problem,
protecting that pressure matters more than recovering the lost hour.

## The room, and the clock

Cool, dark and quiet, with the clock turned away. Checking the time during the night converts
wakefulness into arithmetic about how little sleep remains, which raises arousal exactly when the
aim is to lower it. Exercise supports sleep generally; vigorous exercise very close to bedtime
suits some people and not others, and it is worth testing individually rather than assuming.
