> **Educational summary — not medical advice.** This note is reference material for a software
> project. It does not diagnose or treat, and it must not be used for real medical decisions.

## Call emergency services immediately

- Sudden, severe abdominal pain, particularly if it came on within minutes.
- Abdominal pain with a rigid, board-like or exquisitely tender abdomen.
- Abdominal pain with fever, a fast heart rate, dizziness, fainting, or confusion.
- Abdominal pain with vomiting blood, or black tarry stools.
- Abdominal pain with an inability to pass stool or gas, and a distended abdomen.
- Severe pain in the abdomen or back in someone over 60, particularly with fainting or a pulsating
  abdominal sensation.
- Abdominal or pelvic pain in pregnancy, or in anyone who could be pregnant, with bleeding,
  faintness or shoulder-tip pain.
- Severe pain in the testicle or scrotum of sudden onset, with or without abdominal pain.

## Seek same-day medical assessment

Persistent pain lasting more than a few hours that is worsening, pain that localizes to the right
lower abdomen, pain with persistent vomiting, pain with jaundice or dark urine, and new severe pain
in anyone immunosuppressed, elderly, or recently operated on.

## Why some of these are time-critical

Several abdominal emergencies have a window measured in hours. A twisted testicle loses viability
within about six hours, and the presentation is sudden severe pain — sometimes referred to the
abdomen, which is why it belongs on an abdominal list. An ectopic pregnancy can rupture and bleed
internally, and shoulder-tip pain reflects blood irritating the diaphragm. A leaking abdominal
aortic aneurysm presents as sudden severe abdominal or back pain, often with collapse. A perforated
organ produces the rigid abdomen. Bowel that has lost its blood supply causes pain strikingly out of
proportion to what examination finds — a pattern worth knowing precisely because it invites
under-reaction.

## What not to do

Do not take painkillers, particularly non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, in the hope of settling
severe undiagnosed abdominal pain — they can worsen bleeding and ulceration. Do not eat or drink
while waiting, in case surgery is needed. Do not apply heat to an acutely painful abdomen. Do not
treat presumed constipation with laxatives when the abdomen is distended and nothing is passing,
since obstruction is the concern.

## Groups where the signs are blunted

Older adults, people on corticosteroids or immunosuppressants, people with diabetes, and people in
late pregnancy can have serious intra-abdominal disease with unimpressive examination findings and
little fever. The threshold for assessment in these groups is lower, and a reassuring examination
carries less weight than it otherwise would.
